    DSC criteria

    same rubric as the NeurIPS 2026 DSC Explorer · click to expand

    Each of the CoRL 2026 papers was labelled core, adjacent or out from its title and abstract with the written rubric of the NeurIPS 2026 DSC Explorer, applied unchanged. The question is what the paper contributes, not which words it uses.

    1. Core: the main contribution controls, identifies, estimates or certifies a dynamical system. Subcategories: optimal control & MPC; system identification & learned dynamics for control; stability, safety & certificates; estimation & filtering; learning-based & adaptive control theory; robotics & embodied control (a controller, a dynamics-aware planner, whole-body or contact control, learned dynamics for control, sim-to-real of a controller).
    2. Adjacent: a control researcher would read it, but control is not the contribution. Subcategories: model-based RL & world models; neural ODE/PDE surrogates; dynamical-systems view of learning; time series & dynamical-system reconstruction; sequential decision making close to control (safe, constrained or robust RL, POMDPs, and end-to-end robot policy learning with a generic RL or imitation algorithm).
    3. Out: everything else, including robotics papers on perception, VLA models, datasets or language grounding without a controller contribution, and video “world models” with no planning use.
    4. How: an LLM read every title and abstract against the rubric and gave tier, subcategory and a one-line reason (shown as “Why”). A second, independent pass relabelled 217 papers (every core paper plus 60 random adjacent and 60 random out) without seeing the first labels: tier agreement 81.6% (Cohen's κ 0.72), in/out agreement 92.2%, subcategory agreement 79%. The 40 disagreements were re-read and settled by hand against the rubric; most moved RL-trained humanoid whole-body controllers from core to adjacent, following the rubric's line that robot RL is core only when dynamics or control structure is central. Borders are fuzzy, especially core robotics vs adjacent policy learning, so treat counts as indicative.

    How this was made

    data, method and caveats
    • Papers and sessions come from the official CoRL 2026 accepted-papers page ( papers: orals, spotlights, three poster sessions). Every paper has a poster board; orals and spotlights also have a talk slot. The count on the page, each session's declared size and three independent paper lists (corl-explorer, conference-explorers, corl-2026-papers) all match.
    • Authors, abstracts and keywords are from OpenReview, via the public snapshots in operatic-ai/corl-explorer and gisbi-kim/conference-explorers. Authors are grouped by OpenReview profile ID.
    • Affiliations are per author where possible: first from OpenAlex (looked up by arXiv ID), then read from the author block of the arXiv paper (HTML and PDF first page) for authors OpenAlex left blank. An author is linked to an institution only when the paper ties them to it. For papers with no per-author data, the institution list scraped from the paper's byline by conference-explorers is used at paper level. of author slots have an affiliation, and of papers have at least one institution. Papers not on arXiv often have none, so small institutions are undercounted.
    • Research areas are CoRL's official subject areas (the primary area each paper chose), grouped into . The free-text keywords authors added are shown under each paper.
    • Counting: a paper counts once for every institution with at least one author on it, so shares add up to more than 100%. The fractional count splits each paper equally across its authors, then across each author's institutions.
    • Names are normalized with a hand-checked table (the ~560 institutions of the CDC 2026 Explorer, the NeurIPS rules, and rules for frequent CoRL labs and robotics companies). Google includes DeepMind; all Chinese Academy of Sciences institutes are merged. Unmatched affiliations are kept as written, so a small institution can appear under more than one spelling.
    • Topic search runs entirely in your browser: your text is embedded with bge-small-en-v1.5 (downloaded once, about 33 MB) and compared with every paper's title and abstract. Nothing you type is sent anywhere. If the model can't load, the page falls back to keyword matching on titles.
    • Links: titles open the paper on OpenReview. arXiv links ( papers) and code and project pages ( papers with code) come from the three community lists above, which match by OpenReview ID, title or arXiv ID.
    • Industry vs academia: companies and corporate labs are industry. Universities, government and national labs and public institutes are academia.
    • Download the institution table (CSV) · Download the paper list with authors, affiliations, areas and links (CSV).
    • Spotted a wrong affiliation or mapping? Corrections are welcome.
